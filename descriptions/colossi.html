Giants that carry a real, buildable island on their backs: a tortoise, a whale, a flying jellyfish, a steam centipede, a gel slime and a stone golem. Climb aboard, build a home, take the helm and walk it across the world.

Supports Minecraft 1.21.1 on NeoForge and Fabric. Needs Sable and GeckoLib.

Somewhere out there, a hill is walking. Moss grows on its shoulders, smoke rises from a camp on its back, and a lift cabin swings from its flank, waiting for you.

Colossi adds six enormous creatures that carry real land on their backs: hills, trees, ruins, ponds, and everything you build yourself, all made of ordinary blocks that move with the animal. Climb aboard, make it your home, and once you have claimed one, steer it across the world.

The Zaratan, the giant tortoise

A walking mountain with a shell about 104 blocks long, grown over with flat vines and moss. Small animals live on its back, a ruined jetty with a crane waits on its flank, and a lift takes you up. At night the runes on its shell glow and its head turns to watch you. It is the calmest of the colossi and the best first home.

The Jasconius, the whale

A whale 130 blocks long with a wrecked galleon on its back, a lifeboat as its lift, and pale scars that glow in the dark. It swims the deep ocean with dolphins at its bow, drops anchor when you ask it to, and rises out of the sea in a slow breach.

The Aurelia, the flying jellyfish

A jellyfish the size of a village drifting over the land under a ribbed dome, with a storm observatory on top. 72 curling tentacles reach for anyone who falls and carry them back to the deck. It finds a landing site on its own, and in a storm the sparks crackle on its lightning rods.

The Scolopendra, the steam centipede

A train on legs: a locomotive and eight cars, 220 blocks of brass, copper and glass that bends round corners. Every car is an empty platform for you to fill with a workshop, a greenhouse or cabins. Gears turn along its flanks and searchlights sweep the night.

The Limax, the gel slime

A translucent slime the size of a house that slides over the land and floats on deep water. The inside is empty and yours. Walk in from any side, build behind the glass wall of its skin, and watch the world roll by through the jelly. Magma and honey slimes leave a trail of their own.

The Stone Golem, with its caravan

A hunched stone giant, about 36 blocks tall, covered in moss, vines and glowing runes, dragging a six-wheeled caravan you can build on. Its feet and the wheels level the ground, and its Breakthrough bores a tunnel through a mountain for a short time. (The golem is still work in progress and may change.)

  • A real back. The deck is a physical piece of the world, built on Sable sub-levels. It moves, turns and carries you, your chests and your builds. Blocks you mine from it slowly grow back; what you build is never overwritten.
  • Islands, ruins, a lift. Every colossus gets its own randomly arranged camp, pond, ruin and grove. A lift cabin or a tentacle takes you up, and the colossus slows down while you board.
  • Life aboard. Birds, squirrels, butterflies and frogs live on a Zaratan, gulls and fish on a whale. They keep clear of you and cannot be hurt.

  • Claim it, steer it. Open the console on a wild colossus and it is yours. At the helm the camera follows from behind: W speeds up, S brakes, A and D turn, Space lies it down.
  • Call it back. The Colossus Whistle calls your colossus to you from wherever it is. The Colossus Compass points the way.
  • Walk through the world. A land colossus crushes trees and shaves hills when you steer it at a run, and everything grows back after five minutes. The golem can bore through a mountain; the slime eats the ground it crosses.
  • Keep it loaded. A colossus you own keeps its chunks loaded, even while you are offline, so it can keep walking. You can turn that off.

  • Footfalls you feel. Dust, flung clods and a rolling shockwave at every step, wheel dust behind the caravan, rock chips and a rumble in the golem's tunnel. One setting, effects.intensity, turns all of it up or down.
  • Runes by day and by night. The runes now glow clearly even in daylight, and at night the whole animal lights up.
  • Looks good with your graphics mods. Tested with Sodium, Sodium Extra and Iris with the shader packs Complementary Reimagined and BSL (see the table below). A quality setting (low, medium, high) keeps the frame rate up on weaker machines.

With the Arcane Framework installed you get a book of your own: the Colossi Codex (a book, a core crystal and a block of moss). One page for each colossus, with its portrait, what it can do and how to build on it, plus chapters on steering, resting, the whistle and the terrain. It sits in the Codex Hub next to the books of other mods.

  • Back up your world before you update; this mod changes fast.
  • Wild colossi are rare on purpose. Craft a seed to grow your own (Zaratan, Jasconius, Aurelia).
  • A landing foot hurts. Whatever stands under a Zaratan's foot gets stepped on. Players on the deck are safe.
  • If a colossus makes the game stutter, set quality = "low" in config/colossi-client.toml.

  • Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge 21.1.250 or newer (a Fabric build is also published)
  • Sable 2.0.6 or newer (you install it yourself)
  • GeckoLib 4.9.3 or newer
  • Install on both the client and the server
  • Optional: Create, Serene Seasons, Tempest Almanac, Arcane Framework
